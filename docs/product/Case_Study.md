# Case study

A product manager must explain why a headline gain is insufficient to proceed. Experiment Verdict makes the whole decision contract visible: completion, complaint guardrail, allocation and exposure quality, uncertainty, and limits. The reviewer chooses a verdict and cites specific evidence before committing an immutable snapshot.

The central choice is to keep the original rule as the proceed gate even when a reviewer explores a post-result threshold. A flexible spreadsheet alternative is easier to change but can obscure which rules preceded the result. Here, a relaxed revision remains visibly exploratory and is preserved next to the original.

The deliberately difficult fixture has +8 percentage points completion and +3 points complaints. The original complaint upper bound exceeds +1 point. A reviewer can stop or request another test; the headline cannot unlock proceed. Two invalid quality fixtures and a small sample show why apparently positive numbers may not support inference.

Recovery protects evidence rather than hiding data loss: invalid bytes remain until a readable, explicit reset; stale previews reject; write failures keep work in memory with a reload warning. Withdrawal preserves original evidence and adds a reason.

Mo supplies Product / Program Management direction and portfolio scope. Codex generated the app, original fictional fixtures, product documents and automated tests. Software checks do not establish reviewer comprehension, human validation, commercial impact, real experiment results, or manual coding by Mo.

Proposed study: ask reviewers to recognize invalid evidence, identify harm despite the headline, explain uncertainty and segment limits, then record a consistent verdict. Observe correctness, time to a justified decision, evidence-reference quality, and recovery comprehension. This study has not been run.
